TESTING PROGRAM

Activity and susceptibility testing

Supporting quality testing of carbapenem-resistant Gram-negatives so laboratories can deliver timely, reliable results to the clinical team.

Available susceptibility testing methods

Commercial methods use standard media. No special preparation is required.

Disk diffusion (primary method for most sites)

A 30 µg disk is applied to standard Mueller-Hinton agar using the Kirby-Bauer method. Zone diameters are read in millimetres and interpreted against the applicable breakpoint table.

  • Manufacturers include BD BBL Sensi-Disc, HardyDisk (AST), and Thermo Scientific Oxoid disks.
  • Disks are typically supplied in packs of 50. Once opened they have a limited in-use life.
  • Results in the area of technical uncertainty should not be reported as “not usable”. Confirm with an MIC method where indicated.

Confirmatory MIC methods

When a quantitative result is required, commercial broth microdilution panels are available:

  • ComASP-style panels — broth microdilution kit (Liofilchem).
  • Sensititre AST plates — use standard Sensititre Mueller-Hinton broth; no specialty media.

Access to MIC panels through this program is controlled by hospital code. Most sites will start with disks.

Why activity matters for the laboratory

Spectrum against resistant Gram-negatives

The agent remains active against many carbapenem-resistant isolates by resisting key resistance mechanisms:

  • Stable to Class A (KPC, GES), Class B metallo-β-lactamases (NDM, VIM, IMP), Class C (AmpC), and Class D (OXA-23, OXA-48) enzymes.
  • Retains activity in the presence of porin loss and efflux pump up-regulation.
  • Active against isolates carrying more than one carbapenemase.

Organisms of interest in Australian laboratories include Acinetobacter baumannii complex, Pseudomonas aeruginosa, Klebsiella pneumoniae, Escherichia coli, and Enterobacter cloacae complex, among others.

What the laboratory needs to deliver

  • Early testing of carbapenem-resistant isolates so the clinical team has a result when they need it.
  • Quality zone reading and correct interpretation of the technical-uncertainty range.
  • Access to a confirmatory MIC method for selected sites.

Real-world context

Observational programs (including PERSEUS in Europe and PROVE in the US) have described use in highly resistant Gram-negative infections where other options were limited. These data support the value of reliable susceptibility results in guiding therapy, while reinforcing that in-vitro activity must always be interpreted alongside the clinical picture.

Education only. Local protocols, CLSI/EUCAST guidance, and product information supplied with materials take precedence.
