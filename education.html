TESTING PROGRAM

How susceptibility testing is done

For carbapenem-resistant Gram-negative isolates, additional testing is required beyond automated systems. Disk diffusion is the practical first step for most laboratories.

Why test

Reserved agents are used only after susceptibility is confirmed. If the laboratory does not test, the clinical team cannot know whether the isolate is susceptible. Early, quality testing supports appropriate use and builds laboratory confidence.

Isolates that are resistant to a carbapenem on the automated system should proceed to additional testing for last-line options.

Disk diffusion (primary method)

A 30 µg siderophore cephalosporin disk is placed on standard Mueller-Hinton agar using the Kirby-Bauer method. No special media preparation is required.

  • Read the zone of inhibition in millimetres.
  • Interpret against the relevant breakpoint table (CLSI / EUCAST as applicable in Australia).
  • Results in the area of technical uncertainty may need confirmatory MIC testing.

Disks are supplied in packs of 50. Once opened they have a limited shelf life, so laboratories should order what they can use.

Confirmatory MIC options

When the disk result falls in the area of technical uncertainty, or a quantitative MIC is required, a commercial broth microdilution panel can be used (for example a ComASP-style siderophore cephalosporin panel). Availability of these panels in Australia is limited; the program controls which sites can request them.

Quality points

  • Use quality-control strains appropriate for Gram-negative AST.
  • Follow the manufacturer and CLSI/EUCAST reading guidance for zone diameters.
  • Do not report a disk result as “not usable” solely because it falls in the technical uncertainty range — confirm with MIC where indicated.
